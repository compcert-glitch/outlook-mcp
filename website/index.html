Straight answers

The questions people actually ask us.

Do I need planning permission?
Almost never. Roof-mounted panels on a house are permitted development in England, Scotland and Wales as long as they sit less than 200 mm off the roof plane and don't project above the ridge. Listed buildings, conservation areas and flats are the exceptions — we check your address at survey and handle the application if one is needed.
Will it work on a north-facing or shaded roof?
A due-north roof gives up roughly 45% of the output of a south one, which usually pushes payback past 20 years — we'll normally advise against it. East–west split arrays are a different story: total output drops only around 20%, and the flatter morning-and-evening curve often self-consumes better than a south array does. Set the estimator to "east / west" and compare.
How long do the panels and the battery really last?
Panels carry a 25-year performance warranty, typically guaranteeing 87% output at year 25 — they degrade about 0.5% a year and rarely fail outright. The inverter is the part that wears: budget for one replacement at 12–15 years, currently £1,100–£1,600. An LFP battery is warranted for 10 years or 6,000 cycles, whichever comes first; at one cycle a day that is 16 years of cycling.
What is SEG, and how much is export actually worth?
The Smart Export Guarantee obliges large suppliers to pay you for electricity you send back. Rates are set by each supplier and currently run from about 4p to 16.5p per kWh — a four-fold spread, so the tariff you pick matters more than most people expect. You need a smart meter and an MCS certificate to apply. We don't take commission for recommending a supplier.
Is a battery worth it, or is that the upsell?
On its own bill savings alone, a 10.4 kWh battery pays back in roughly 9–13 years — slower than the panels. It becomes clearly worth it in three cases: high evening use, an off-peak tariff you can charge from cheaply, or you want the array to keep working in a power cut. If none of those is you, we'll say so and quote the PV alone.
Can my roof take the weight?
A framed array adds around 12–15 kg/m², well inside what a standard rafter-and-purlin roof carries. The things that stop a job are rotten battens, failed felt or a roof due to be re-covered within five years — all found at survey. If the roof needs work, do it first; stripping and refitting an array afterwards costs £1,400–£2,200.
What happens if you go out of business?
Your 10-year workmanship warranty is insurance-backed through an IWA policy, so it is not dependent on us existing. Panel, inverter and battery warranties sit with the manufacturers and transfer with the property. Your RECC membership also gives you deposit protection before the work starts.
Do I pay VAT?
Domestic solar, battery storage and the labour to install them are zero-rated for VAT in the UK until 31 March 2027. Batteries retrofitted to an existing array are included. Every price on this page is a final price with 0% VAT applied — there is nothing to add at the end.
